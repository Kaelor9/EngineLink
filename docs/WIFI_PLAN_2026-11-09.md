Two facts stand out:

1. **Router mode has worked on this board**, for hours, on the boat router
   (0.13.4 and 0.15.0: station power save off, HT20, 20 s beacon timeout).
2. **On the home 4G router no build has ever served the page**, with power
   save on or off, with HT20 or the driver's default, with or without the WPA3
   wrapper and the TCP window patch. Every session there ends the same way:
   the station stops receiving (ARP/ping/TCP from the PC lost, beacons lost,
   reason 200), then 49 full scans in two minutes do not see the router at all,
   while the own Wi-Fi started right after hears the phone at −40 dBm, and the
   next attempt later finds the router in under a second.

### 1.2 Why the router link is obtained but HTTP does not work

Association and DHCP are short exchanges that the station starts itself. The
HTTPS report #162 (TLS handshake with GitHub, several kilobytes **received**)
proves that on 2026-11-06 the station's own TCP traffic worked through that
router at +122 s. What fails is traffic that **another device starts**: the
PC's ARP request, ICMP echo, TCP SYN. On 2026-11-08 one ping in ten was
answered; on 2026-11-09 none. A request that never reaches lwIP produces a
browser timeout, exactly what the owner saw (`ERR_CONNECTION_TIMED_OUT`, not
a refused connection and not a 421/403 from the server's own checks).

Nothing in the firmware can produce this selectively:

- `esp_http_server` binds `INADDR_ANY` and is started once (`httpd.ino:220`);
  it answers 1 676 external requests in the same boot in standalone mode and
  the loopback self-test passes in router mode. Its Host/Origin checks answer
  421/403; they cannot cause a timeout.
- lwIP routes replies on the interface that received the request; the idle AP
  netif (192.168.4.1) does not interfere with 192.168.0.x.
- No scan, mode change or driver call runs in router mode except
  `esp_wifi_sta_get_ap_info()` every 2 s (`wifi.ino:249`), which is a local
  read. The main loop's longest pass was 111 ms; the web server runs in its
  own task. There is no concurrency or timing path that drops received frames.

The one setting in `75faba4` that **does** match the symptom is modem sleep:
`WiFi.setSleep(true)` (`wifi.ino:197`, commit `b3ed536`), confirmed by
`"powerSave":1` in the report. `esp_wifi.h` says the default is
`WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM`; the Wi-Fi guide says reception is then delayed to the
DTIM cycle, and `WIFI_PS_NONE` "minimizes the delay in receiving Wi-Fi data".
Espressif issue 12454 shows precisely "got IP, unreachable, `bcn_timeout`"
under `WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM`. Modem sleep is therefore a real defect of the latest
build for a device that must answer unsolicited requests. **But it is not the
whole cause**: 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-08 failed with power save off.

### 1.3 Why the station later loses beacons

Per the Wi-Fi guide ("Wi-Fi Beacon Timeout", ESP32-S3): if no beacon arrives
within the inactive time (default 6 s, `esp_wifi_set_inactive_time()`), the
station sends 5 probe requests; with no answer it disconnects with reason 200.
So reason 200 means **the station heard nothing from the router for about
6–8 s**, at −59 dBm (2026-11-09) and −42 dBm (2026-11-08): not a weak-signal
value. The 49 × 201 that follow are the core's reconnect
(`STA.cpp:158–173`: `disconnect()` + `connect()` after every failure, a full
scan each ~2.4 s) finding **no beacon or probe response from that router on
any channel for two minutes**, until the 120 s fallback starts the own Wi-Fi.
The same two-minute blindness appears at power-on (#95 and 2026-11-09) and
ended both times by itself.

Scanning runs with the radio awake, so power save cannot explain the two
minutes. No firmware activity runs during them either. What remains is below
the firmware's logic: the radio downlink from that router to this board at
that bench. Three candidates are consistent with all sessions:

- **R1, the router's 2.4 GHz radio**: a 4G router (TP-Link Archer MR600,
  dual band) whose 2.4 GHz radio restarts, moves channel (auto) or is held
  back by LTE/Wi-Fi coexistence, while the PC and the iPhone stay on 5 GHz
  and notice nothing. The owner has not yet checked which band the PC uses.
- **R2, the place**: interference at the board's position on channel 1 (the
  4G router's own LTE uplink bursts next to 2.4 GHz, USB 3.0 noise from the PC
  the board was attached to, or a neighbour). Time-varying, so sessions differ.
- **R3, the board's receive path**: a sensitivity loss (antenna matching, RF
  keepout, a supply transient under receive) that −40 dBm from a phone a metre
  away hides and −59 dBm from a router does not. The IC5 repair restored the
  3.3 V rail and standalone throughput; it says nothing about receive margin.

None of these is a firmware fault, and no firmware change can repair any of
them. Section 5 shows how one bench session tells them apart without a single
extra firmware build.

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Architecture

- **Keep** the Arduino core 3.3.12 build with Espressif's components used
  directly (`esp_wifi`, `esp_netif`, `esp_http_server`, `mdns`, OTA). The
  server rewrite works and is not part of the problem.
- **Keep two user-selectable modes only**: Standalone (AP only) and Router
  (STA only). The automatic fallback to the own Wi-Fi after 120 s without an
  IP, and the automatic return while no phone is joined, stay **as a recovery
  mechanism**, named so in the report and the app, never as a mode.
- **Drop Wi-Fi 2.0 Stage 4** as designed: no `WIFI_AP_STA` idle station, no
  router test with the AP up, no periodic "looks", no `wifistate::Machine`.
  The simple exclusive-mode `wifi.ino` of 0.16.2–0.16.6 is the right size for
  the product; its only shortcoming is the station profile (F1–F2).
- **Station = the ESP32 driver's defaults, as the Prism controller** (2.2,
  revision 2). No setting on the station interface at all. No linker
  wrappers, no private lwIP, no custom SAE.

### 2.2 The station: the Prism controller's connection path (revision 2, 2026-11-09 evening)

**Why revised.** The rc1 bench report (built 15:37:56) on the home 4G router:
49 × 201 at boot, then after the app's Connect about 24 × reason 2
(AUTH_EXPIRE: auth request sent, no answer), 6 × reason 4 (association
request sent, no answer), 2 × 15 (EAPOL 3/4 never arrived), 1 × 202. Per the
Wi-Fi guide's connect phases every one of these is "EngineLink sent, the
router's answer never arrived". The owner reported that the Prism controller
(`Kaelor9/Marine-RGB-Light-Controller`, the same ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N8R8
module, Arduino core latest at build time) joins the same router without
trouble, and asked for Prism's connection method in router mode.

**Comparison on the actual code** (Prism `connectWiFi()`/`setup()`/`loop()`;
WiFiManager 2.0.17 `autoConnect` → `connectWifi` → `wifiConnectDefault` →
`WiFi.begin()` → `waitForConnectResult`; EngineLink `applyNetwork()`,
`serviceNetwork()`, `onWiFi()`):

| Step | Prism (effective, after WiFiManager) | EngineLink rc1 | rc2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Init | `WiFi.mode(WIFI_STA)`, `setHostname`; WM turns Wi-Fi **OFF** again (hostname branch) and `enableSTA(true)` | `WiFi.mode(WIFI_OFF)`, `persistent(false)`, `setHostname`, `WiFi.mode(WIFI_STA)` | same in effect, kept |
| Country | `WiFiSetCountry()` skipped (`_wificountry==""`): driver default "01" | `esp_wifi_set_country_code("DK",true)` on the station | **removed on the station**; the AP keeps DK |
| Power save | driver default `WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM` | `WiFi.setSleep(false)` | **removed** |
| Bandwidth, scan | driver defaults (40 MHz allowed, fast scan) | HT20 + `WIFI_ALL_CHANNEL_SCAN` | **removed** |
| Credentials | `persistent(true)` only when saving; `WiFi.begin()` uses the driver's NVS config | `persistent(false)`, `WiFi.begin(ssid,pass)` from EngineLink's settings | same for the driver (`esp_wifi_set_config` + `connect`), kept |
| Reconnect | the core's (`setAutoReconnect(true)`) | the core's | same, kept |
| Events | WM's `WiFiEvent` (status only) | `onWiFi()` (counters/trace only, no Wi-Fi calls) | same in effect, kept for the report |
| Waiting for an IP | blocking 10 s (`setConnectTimeout`), else portal → `ESP.restart()` | non-blocking in `serviceNetwork()`; 120 s without an IP → own Wi-Fi (recovery), retry every 10 min | **not transferred**: EngineLink must not block engine reading or restart; recovery kept |
| mDNS | `MDNS.begin` once after the IP | `startNameService()` 2 s after GOT_IP, restarted on a network change | same in effect, kept |
| Web server | Arduino `WebServer` after the IP, `handleClient()` in the loop | `esp_http_server`, started once, all interfaces | kept (one server in both modes) |
| While connected | nothing polls the driver | `esp_wifi_sta_get_ap_info()` every 2 s | **removed**; the signal is read only when a report or the app asks |
| Standalone | WM captive portal | EngineLink's SoftAP, `apRadioSetup()` | unchanged |

Transferred: the bare driver defaults on the station, nothing polling the
driver while it connects, the core's reconnect, mDNS after the IP. Not
transferred: WiFiManager (portal, blocking wait, restart), NVS-stored
credentials (no RF effect), WM's event handler.

**Critical assessment.** The chance that this alone fixes the home router is
low: `75faba4` (2026-11-09 morning) already ran the driver's defaults with
modem sleep on and failed the same way. What rc2 gives is final: a router
path identical to a device known to work on the same module. If it still
fails identically, the firmware's connection path is excluded with
certainty and the board, its supplies or the place remain (section 5.4,
hardware path). If it works, one of the small differences mattered and the
code is simpler. Prism's core version is "latest at build time": if Prism was
built on core 2.x (IDF 4.4), the driver version is a real difference and one
EngineLink build on that core is the next software step.

## 4. The hardware finding

The IC5 cold joint explains the earlier 3.3 V and standalone-throughput
variability (65–1 048 kB/s before the repair, 540–1 105 kB/s after). It is
recorded as fixed in `docs/HARDWARE.md` and `docs/STATUS.md` (P6). It does
**not** explain the router failures: they look the same before and after the
repair, and the 2026-11-08 session failed at −42 dBm.

The plan therefore does not assume the board is cleared. Section 5 separates
the board from the router and the place in the same session that verifies the
firmware. If the board is implicated (outcome C below), the next step is
hardware work from `docs/HARDWARE.md` (antenna matching and keepout, a scope
on 3.3 V during a router session), not another firmware version.

## 5. Verification: one build, one bench session, then the boat

### 5.1 Before the session (owner, 5 minutes, no firmware involved)

1. On the Windows PC on the home router: `netsh wlan show interfaces` and
   note **Radio type, Band/Channel**. If the PC is on 5 GHz, its good
   experience says nothing about the 2.4 GHz radio EngineLink uses.
2. Router admin page, read only, no changes: 2.4 GHz channel setting
   (auto/fixed), channel width, whether Smart Connect / band steering, Airtime
   Fairness or AP Isolation are on, and the system log. Keep a screenshot.

### 5.2 The session (owner, about 40 minutes, firmware 0.17.0-rc2 from CI)

Board at its usual bench position, lab supply, **not** USB-attached to the PC
(USB 3.0 noise is one of the R2 candidates). Start these on the PC first and
leave them running the whole time:

```bat
ping -t 192.168.0.2 > ping.txt
```

```bat
:loop
echo %time% >> scan.txt
netsh wlan show networks mode=bssid | findstr /i "SSID BSSID Signal Channel" >> scan.txt
timeout /t 10 > nul
goto loop
```

Then in the app: Router mode, the saved home router. Within the first
5 minutes, from the PC: 20 × `http://192.168.0.2/api/live`, 10 × `http://192.168.0.2/`,
3 × `http://enginelink.local/`. Let it run to 30 minutes, then save the Wi-Fi
report and `/WIFI.LOG` from the SD card.

In the same session, as a control, put the Prism controller (another ESP32,
driver defaults) on the same router at the same spot and `ping -t` it from
the PC for 15 minutes.

### 5.3 Pass criteria (all required)

| Criterion | Pass |
|---|---|
| Pings to EngineLink | ≥ 99 of 100 answered in any 100-ping window |
| `/api/live` | 20 of 20 within 2 s |
| `/` | 10 of 10 complete within 5 s |
| `enginelink.local` | resolves on the PC (if Windows lacks mDNS, the IP checks count) |
| Report | 30 min, 0 unexpected disconnects (no reason 200), 0 fallbacks, `Power save: off`, country `DK`, `802.11n 20 MHz` |
| Standalone | unchanged: page loads, speed test ≥ 500 kB/s at −40 dBm |

### 5.4 Reading a failure (one session decides, no further builds)

| Outcome | What `scan.txt` and the Prism control show | Conclusion | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | The PC also lost the router's 2.4 GHz BSSID, or saw it change channel, around EngineLink's beacon loss; Prism dropped too | R1, the router's 2.4 GHz radio | Firmware is done. The owner decides about the router (it is the test case, not the target, D30); the boat test proceeds |
| B | The PC saw the 2.4 GHz BSSID steady on channel 1 at good signal; **Prism also** lost pings at that spot | R2, the place | Move the bench position once; firmware unchanged; boat test proceeds |
| C | The PC saw the BSSID steady; **Prism stayed at 100 %** while EngineLink failed | R3, the board | Stop firmware work. Hardware investigation per section 4 |
| D | Everything passes | profile + link fine | Boat test (5.5), then release 0.17.0 |

### 5.5 Boat (the real acceptance)

One trip with the boat router: the same criteria as 5.3 from the iPhone
(`/api/live` via the app, `/` three times, 30 min without a fallback),
plus one router power-cycle: reconnect within 60 s after the router is back,
no fallback when it returns within 2 min. Standalone checked once on the same
day. Release 0.17.0 only after this.

| TCP port 80 | one `esp_http_server` started once in `setup()`, bound to every address (`INADDR_ANY`), never stopped; a network change only closes sessions (`httpdSuspend()`); it serves the own Wi-Fi again after each router session in the same start, so the listener survives the changes | no fault found |
| HTTP checks | wrong `Host` → 421, foreign `Origin` → 403, POST without `X-EngineLink` → 403: all are **answers**; none can produce a timeout. 0 refused in the report | no fault found |
| Port 443 | nothing listens; lwIP answers a connection attempt with a reset at once | by design |
| mDNS | `MDNS.begin` 2 s after each IP, restarted on a network change; queries are multicast and travel like the ARP request | depends on the same reception |
| Tasks, locks | Wi-Fi task priority 23 and lwIP 18 on core 0; engine tasks 3–4 with a yield every pass; web server in its own task on core 1; longest main-loop pass 175 ms; the event callback makes no Wi-Fi call | no starvation path |
| Web app | never reached: the browser gets no TCP connection | not involved |

So the browser's timeout is **missing network traffic, not a missing HTTP
answer and not the web app**: frames from the router do not reach EngineLink's
IP stack reliably, while frames EngineLink asks for itself mostly do. The same
reception fails completely for 6+ s when the beacons are lost, and the 49
scans that follow hear nothing of that router for two minutes.

Why Standalone works and Router does not: in Standalone the phone is a metre
away, EngineLink sets the pace on its own channel (6) and every frame is
addressed and acknowledged. In Router mode everything another device starts
arrives via the router, first as a broadcast (ARP, mDNS) that the router sends
once, unacknowledged, at its lowest rate, right after a beacon. That is the
part of the link that is failing.

### 8.2 What was wrong in rc2 (found in the source, not guessed)

1. **The station's power save differed between sessions.** rc2 made no
   power-save call on the station, but the access point setup calls
   `WiFi.setSleep(false)`, the core stores it (`WiFiGeneric.cpp:784`) and
   applies the stored value at every station start (`STA.cpp:122`). A start
   straight into router mode ran modem sleep **on**; any session after the own
   Wi-Fi had been up ran it **off**. In the rc2 report the modem-sleep session
   lost the router by itself after 409 s (and the 2026-11-09 morning build
   with modem sleep on after 539 s); the session without ran 769 s until the
   owner switched the router off, and 1105 s on 2026-11-06. Too few sessions
   to prove a cause, but modem sleep makes a server depend on catching one
   specific beacon for every incoming packet, on a link whose beacons are the
   very thing being lost.
2. **The report's "Power save" and HTTP figures could not be attributed.**
   The power-save line was read in access point mode, and the HTTP counters
   mixed the router, the own Wi-Fi and the self-test. "4 send failures" and
   "slowest `/` 10.9 s" may be a page trickling over the router or sessions
   closed by a network change; rc2 cannot say.
3. **The RF calibration is older than the regulator repair.** The driver
   calibrates the radio in full once, stores the result and reuses it at every
   start ("partial calibration", Espressif's RF calibration guide). The stored
   data was made while IC5 had a cold solder joint and the 3.3 V rail was
   unstable. Updates over Wi-Fi keep it. Whether it is bad is unknown; it is
   the one radio input that was never renewed after the repair.
4. **`http://enginelink/` was refused.** Many routers register the DHCP host
   name in their own DNS, so the bare name (or `enginelink.lan`) resolves on a
   PC where `.local` does not; the `Host` check answered 421.
5. **CI's version check rejected `-rc2`** (`validate.yml` allowed letters only
   after the dash).

### 8.3 `http://` and `https://`

EngineLink serves HTTP on port 80 only, in both modes, and the address is the
same in both: `http://enginelink.local`. Nothing in the firmware or the app
redirects to or expects HTTPS. Current browsers try `https://` first for a
typed name and fall back to `http://` when port 443 is refused; EngineLink
refuses at once, so on a working link the fallback takes milliseconds. On a
link that drops packets the refusal is lost too, the browser waits out its
HTTPS attempt and the address bar keeps the `https://` it tried. That is the
most likely source of "it seemed to need https": the same lost packets, seen
through the browser's upgrade. A certificate for a local name is not possible
without warnings, so HTTPS on the device is not the conventional answer;
making the link carry packets is.

`.local` needs multicast between the PC and EngineLink. It fails when the
router isolates wireless clients (guest network, "AP isolation"), when a VPN
or firewall on the PC blocks UDP 5353, or when the link drops the multicast.
The IP address always works when the link does, and with 8.2 item 4 so does
the name the router's own DNS gives (`http://enginelink/` on routers that
register it). The report shows the last router IP for that purpose.

### 9.3 Probable cause of the dead data path, and the hypotheses left

**Probable (acted on): the 40 MHz link.** Beacons are sent on the primary
20 MHz channel at the lowest rate and arrived for 302 s at −47 dBm; data
frames in an 802.11n 40 MHz link use both halves, and the upper half
(around channel 5) overlaps whatever the neighbours on channels 3–9 send,
one of them at −50 dBm at the board. Espressif's Wi-Fi driver guide
("Wi-Fi HT20/40") says: "if the device is used in some special environment,
e.g., there are too many other Wi-Fi devices around the ESP32-S3 device, the
performance of HT40 may be degraded. So if the applications need to support
same or similar scenarios, it is recommended that the bandwidth is always
configured to HT20." EngineLink needs under 1 MB/s; a marina is such an
environment. rc4 sets the station to 20 MHz.

What speaks against 40 MHz being the whole story: on 2026-11-06 the link
negotiated 20 MHz and small answers worked but the page did not; rc1 had
20 MHz and never got an answer to its authentication. So 20 MHz is expected
to help and is not proven to be enough.

**Hypotheses that remain, and what rc4 records for each:**

| | Hypothesis | What would show it in the rc4 report |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | The router's 802.11n data frames (rates, aggregation) do not reach this client although its beacons do | radio samples: beacons heard ≈ expected, few frames for EngineLink or none at 802.11n; the 802.11g session (9.4) clearly better than the 802.11n one |
| H2 | The router does not hear EngineLink (EngineLink's transmit quality, or the router's own receiver on channel 1: its 4G modem can transmit in band 40, 2300–2400 MHz, next to channel 1) | radio samples: frames for EngineLink arrive mostly as **repeats** (the router did not get the acknowledgement); pings lost in both profiles; rc1's unanswered authentication fits |
| H3 | The router's 2.4 GHz radio stops for minutes (restart, channel or width change) | beacons heard far below expected before a loss; channel/width change events; scans after the loss: other networks heard, also on the router's channel, the router not |
| H4 | The board's receiver on the router's channel (place, interference, hardware) | scans: networks heard on other channels but none on the router's; noise floor on the router's channel high; Standalone on channel 1 clearly worse than on 6 (no firmware needed) |

Not a finding: group cipher TKIP. It only means the router runs WPA/WPA2
mixed; unicast is CCMP, so 802.11n is allowed, and the ESP32 supports it.
TKIP integrity failures cannot be read from the driver (no public API).

Reviewed again and without finding: DHCP (1.7 s), the address and gateway
(now printed per session), routing between the two interfaces, the web

so this is measured next time instead of argued. Hardware: nothing new;
the module's antenna overhangs the board edge as it should, the IC5 repair
is unchanged, the RF calibration is renewed. H2 and H4 include the board
and cannot be excluded from here.

## 13. 2026-11-10 evening: the bench sketch on the board, and 0.17.0-rc7

**Bench sketch on the home router (owner, screenshots; no EngineLink code,
Arduino `WebServer`, core 3.2.0, channel 1, −43…−48 dBm):**

| Profile | Time | Pings answered | Typical reply | 110 KB page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0, driver defaults (802.11n 40 MHz) | 2 min | 117 of 117 | 10–50 ms | 5 of 5, 1.8–2.2 s |
| 0 | 3.5 min | 205 of 205 | more at 50–200 ms | 3.6–4.0 s |
| 0 | 10 min | 602 of 613 | 153 at 200–1000 ms | **15–16 s**, still delivered (17 of 17) |
| 1, 802.11g only, flashed right after | 30 s | 16 of 25 | 9 at 200–1000 ms | none completed; the page froze |

What it shows:

- The plain sketch degrades on this router too: the same page takes eight
  times longer after 10 minutes at an unchanged signal. So the cause of the
  degrading link is **not in EngineLink's code** (hypothesis C of section 12
  is out) and **not 802.11n** (profile 1 was worse; hypothesis A is out).
- Profile 1 was bad from its first second although the ESP32 had just been
  reset: whatever degrades is not reset with the chip. That leaves the
  board outside the chip (heat, the 3.3 V supply) or the router/place.
- **One real difference in EngineLink's favour to fix:** the sketch still
  delivered the page at 15 s, because `WebServer` waits. EngineLink's server
  gave up after 5 s without progress (`send_wait_timeout = 5`); rc6's five
  failed pages ended after 5.6–26 s.

**Hardware suspicion (documented, not proven):** the 3.3 V rail comes from
IC5 (TLV76733 LDO from 5.24 V, about 0.4–0.7 W of heat under Wi-Fi
transmission, next to the module; the part with the cold joint repaired on
2026-11-09). A regulator that limits when warm would give exactly: good at
first, worse with time and with traffic, still bad after a chip reset, good
again after a pause. `hardware/PCB_GERBER_REVIEW_2026-11-05.md` lists IC5's
temperature and the rail under Wi-Fi traffic as never measured. Checks: the
3.3 V rail on the oscilloscope at the module while `/big` loads after
10 minutes; a finger on IC5; bench sketch profile 2 (8.5 dBm).

**0.17.0-rc7:**

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| `send_wait_timeout` 5 s → 30 s | the page arrives on a slow link instead of being abandoned (above) |
| CI back on core 3.3.12 | rc6 on 3.2.0 failed the same way; the core is cleared, the project's pin is restored (the certificate call in `updates.ino` works on both) |
| station, recovery, Standalone | unchanged from rc5 (the working sketch's three calls) |
