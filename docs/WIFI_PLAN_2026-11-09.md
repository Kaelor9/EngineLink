Two facts stand out:

1. **Router mode has worked on this board**, for hours, on the boat router
   (0.13.4 and 0.15.0: station power save off, HT20, 20 s beacon timeout).
2. **On the home 4G router no build has ever served the page**, with power
   save on or off, with HT20 or the driver's default, with or without the WPA3
   wrapper and the TCP window patch. Every session there ends the same way:
   the station stops receiving (ARP/ping/TCP from the PC lost, beacons lost,
   reason 200), then 49 full scans in two minutes do not see the router at all,
   while the own Wi-Fi started right after hears the phone at −40 dBm, and the
   next attempt later finds the router in under a second.

### 1.2 Why the router link is obtained but HTTP does not work

Association and DHCP are short exchanges that the station starts itself. The
HTTPS report #162 (TLS handshake with GitHub, several kilobytes **received**)
proves that on 2026-11-06 the station's own TCP traffic worked through that
router at +122 s. What fails is traffic that **another device starts**: the
PC's ARP request, ICMP echo, TCP SYN. On 2026-11-08 one ping in ten was
answered; on 2026-11-09 none. A request that never reaches lwIP produces a
browser timeout, exactly what the owner saw (`ERR_CONNECTION_TIMED_OUT`, not
a refused connection and not a 421/403 from the server's own checks).

Nothing in the firmware can produce this selectively:

- `esp_http_server` binds `INADDR_ANY` and is started once (`httpd.ino:220`);
  it answers 1 676 external requests in the same boot in standalone mode and
  the loopback self-test passes in router mode. Its Host/Origin checks answer
  421/403; they cannot cause a timeout.
- lwIP routes replies on the interface that received the request; the idle AP
  netif (192.168.4.1) does not interfere with 192.168.0.x.
- No scan, mode change or driver call runs in router mode except
  `esp_wifi_sta_get_ap_info()` every 2 s (`wifi.ino:249`), which is a local
  read. The main loop's longest pass was 111 ms; the web server runs in its
  own task. There is no concurrency or timing path that drops received frames.

The one setting in `75faba4` that **does** match the symptom is modem sleep:
`WiFi.setSleep(true)` (`wifi.ino:197`, commit `b3ed536`), confirmed by
`"powerSave":1` in the report. `esp_wifi.h` says the default is
`WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM`; the Wi-Fi guide says reception is then delayed to the
DTIM cycle, and `WIFI_PS_NONE` "minimizes the delay in receiving Wi-Fi data".
Espressif issue 12454 shows precisely "got IP, unreachable, `bcn_timeout`"
under `WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM`. Modem sleep is therefore a real defect of the latest
build for a device that must answer unsolicited requests. **But it is not the
whole cause**: 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-08 failed with power save off.

### 1.3 Why the station later loses beacons

Per the Wi-Fi guide ("Wi-Fi Beacon Timeout", ESP32-S3): if no beacon arrives
within the inactive time (default 6 s, `esp_wifi_set_inactive_time()`), the
station sends 5 probe requests; with no answer it disconnects with reason 200.
So reason 200 means **the station heard nothing from the router for about
6–8 s**, at −59 dBm (2026-11-09) and −42 dBm (2026-11-08): not a weak-signal
value. The 49 × 201 that follow are the core's reconnect
(`STA.cpp:158–173`: `disconnect()` + `connect()` after every failure, a full
scan each ~2.4 s) finding **no beacon or probe response from that router on
any channel for two minutes**, until the 120 s fallback starts the own Wi-Fi.
The same two-minute blindness appears at power-on (#95 and 2026-11-09) and
ended both times by itself.

Scanning runs with the radio awake, so power save cannot explain the two
minutes. No firmware activity runs during them either. What remains is below
the firmware's logic: the radio downlink from that router to this board at
that bench. Three candidates are consistent with all sessions:

- **R1, the router's 2.4 GHz radio**: a 4G router (TP-Link Archer MR600,
  dual band) whose 2.4 GHz radio restarts, moves channel (auto) or is held
  back by LTE/Wi-Fi coexistence, while the PC and the iPhone stay on 5 GHz
  and notice nothing. The owner has not yet checked which band the PC uses.
- **R2, the place**: interference at the board's position on channel 1 (the
  4G router's own LTE uplink bursts next to 2.4 GHz, USB 3.0 noise from the PC
  the board was attached to, or a neighbour). Time-varying, so sessions differ.
- **R3, the board's receive path**: a sensitivity loss (antenna matching, RF
  keepout, a supply transient under receive) that −40 dBm from a phone a metre
  away hides and −59 dBm from a router does not. The IC5 repair restored the
  3.3 V rail and standalone throughput; it says nothing about receive margin.

None of these is a firmware fault, and no firmware change can repair any of
them. Section 5 shows how one bench session tells them apart without a single
extra firmware build.

(a loopback request confirms the image, so a bad router link cannot trigger a
rollback), the 120 s fallback and 10 min return logic.

## 2. Decision

### 2.1 Architecture

- **Keep** the Arduino core 3.3.12 build with Espressif's components used
  directly (`esp_wifi`, `esp_netif`, `esp_http_server`, `mdns`, OTA). The
  server rewrite works and is not part of the problem.
- **Keep two user-selectable modes only**: Standalone (AP only) and Router
  (STA only). The automatic fallback to the own Wi-Fi after 120 s without an
  IP, and the automatic return while no phone is joined, stay **as a recovery
  mechanism**, named so in the report and the app, never as a mode.
- **Drop Wi-Fi 2.0 Stage 4** as designed: no `WIFI_AP_STA` idle station, no
  router test with the AP up, no periodic "looks", no `wifistate::Machine`.
  The simple exclusive-mode `wifi.ino` of 0.16.2–0.16.6 is the right size for
  the product; its only shortcoming is the station profile (F1–F2).
- **Station profile = Espressif's documented APIs, three settings, nothing
  else.** Every other parameter stays at the core's and driver's default (PMF
  capable, WPA2 minimum security, WPA3-SAE negotiated by the driver, fast
  reconnect, 20 dBm). No linker wrappers, no private lwIP, no custom SAE, no
  TX-power or beacon-timeout tuning on the station.

### 2.2 The station profile and why each line is there

| Call (after `WiFi.mode(WIFI_STA)`, before `WiFi.begin()`) | Why |
|---|---|
| `WiFi.setSleep(false)` → `WIFI_PS_NONE` | Wi-Fi guide: power save delays reception to the DTIM cycle; `WIFI_PS_NONE` minimises receive delay. EngineLink is powered by the boat, serves unsolicited requests and must not depend on the router's PS buffering. The only configuration that ever worked for hours on this board had it off. |
| `esp_wifi_set_country_code("DK", true)` | `esp_wifi.h` attention 6/8: ISO code from the supported list, 802.11d on so the station follows the router's country element. Set in **both** modes (the driver is deinitialised at every mode switch), so the station scans channels 1–13 actively and runs the same PHY data as the AP. |
| `esp_wifi_set_bandwidth(WIFI_IF_STA, WIFI_BW_HT20)` | Proven part of the working 0.13.4/0.15.0 profile; the home router negotiated 20 MHz anyway (#162). 40 MHz in 2.4 GHz needs a clean secondary channel; HT20 costs nothing EngineLink needs (its best measured throughput is 1.1 MB/s). The one line in this table that may be dropped if the owner prefers pure defaults. |

Plus one core API for router independence (plan I12, D30):
`WiFi.setScanMethod(WIFI_ALL_CHANNEL_SCAN)` so a mesh or repeater network with
several BSSIDs joins the strongest one (`STA.cpp:239` defaults to
`WIFI_FAST_SCAN`, which takes the first match). Cost: about one second more
per join.

Reconnect stays the core's own (`WiFi.setAutoReconnect(true)`): one
`disconnect()`+`connect()` after each failure, from the core's event task. It
is official, already contract-tested, and the IDF guide's own recommendation
of an application strategy buys nothing here that the 120 s fallback does not
already provide.

## 4. The hardware finding

The IC5 cold joint explains the earlier 3.3 V and standalone-throughput
variability (65–1 048 kB/s before the repair, 540–1 105 kB/s after). It is
recorded as fixed in `docs/HARDWARE.md` and `docs/STATUS.md` (P6). It does
**not** explain the router failures: they look the same before and after the
repair, and the 2026-11-08 session failed at −42 dBm.

The plan therefore does not assume the board is cleared. Section 5 separates
the board from the router and the place in the same session that verifies the
firmware. If the board is implicated (outcome C below), the next step is
hardware work from `docs/HARDWARE.md` (antenna matching and keepout, a scope
on 3.3 V during a router session), not another firmware version.

## 5. Verification: one build, one bench session, then the boat

### 5.1 Before the session (owner, 5 minutes, no firmware involved)

1. On the Windows PC on the home router: `netsh wlan show interfaces` and
   note **Radio type, Band/Channel**. If the PC is on 5 GHz, its good
   experience says nothing about the 2.4 GHz radio EngineLink uses.
2. Router admin page, read only, no changes: 2.4 GHz channel setting
   (auto/fixed), channel width, whether Smart Connect / band steering, Airtime
   Fairness or AP Isolation are on, and the system log. Keep a screenshot.

### 5.2 The session (owner, about 40 minutes, firmware 0.17.0-rc1 from CI)

Board at its usual bench position, lab supply, **not** USB-attached to the PC
(USB 3.0 noise is one of the R2 candidates). Start these on the PC first and
leave them running the whole time:

```bat
ping -t 192.168.0.2 > ping.txt
```

```bat
:loop
echo %time% >> scan.txt
netsh wlan show networks mode=bssid | findstr /i "SSID BSSID Signal Channel" >> scan.txt
timeout /t 10 > nul
goto loop
```

Then in the app: Router mode, the saved home router. Within the first
5 minutes, from the PC: 20 × `http://192.168.0.2/api/live`, 10 × `http://192.168.0.2/`,
3 × `http://enginelink.local/`. Let it run to 30 minutes, then save the Wi-Fi
report and `/WIFI.LOG` from the SD card.

In the same session, as a control, put the Prism controller (another ESP32,
driver defaults) on the same router at the same spot and `ping -t` it from
the PC for 15 minutes.

### 5.3 Pass criteria (all required)

| Criterion | Pass |
|---|---|
| Pings to EngineLink | ≥ 99 of 100 answered in any 100-ping window |
| `/api/live` | 20 of 20 within 2 s |
| `/` | 10 of 10 complete within 5 s |
| `enginelink.local` | resolves on the PC (if Windows lacks mDNS, the IP checks count) |
| Report | 30 min, 0 unexpected disconnects (no reason 200), 0 fallbacks, `Power save: off`, country `DK`, `802.11n 20 MHz` |
| Standalone | unchanged: page loads, speed test ≥ 500 kB/s at −40 dBm |

### 5.4 Reading a failure (one session decides, no further builds)

| Outcome | What `scan.txt` and the Prism control show | Conclusion | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | The PC also lost the router's 2.4 GHz BSSID, or saw it change channel, around EngineLink's beacon loss; Prism dropped too | R1, the router's 2.4 GHz radio | Firmware is done. The owner decides about the router (it is the test case, not the target, D30); the boat test proceeds |
| B | The PC saw the 2.4 GHz BSSID steady on channel 1 at good signal; **Prism also** lost pings at that spot | R2, the place | Move the bench position once; firmware unchanged; boat test proceeds |
| C | The PC saw the BSSID steady; **Prism stayed at 100 %** while EngineLink failed | R3, the board | Stop firmware work. Hardware investigation per section 4 |
| D | Everything passes | profile + link fine | Boat test (5.5), then release 0.17.0 |

### 5.5 Boat (the real acceptance)

One trip with the boat router: the same criteria as 5.3 from the iPhone
(`/api/live` via the app, `/` three times, 30 min without a fallback),
plus one router power-cycle: reconnect within 60 s after the router is back,
no fallback when it returns within 2 min. Standalone checked once on the same
day. Release 0.17.0 only after this.

## 6. Out of scope (deliberately)

- No AP+STA concurrent operation, no router test while the own Wi-Fi is up,
  no "Router is back, switch now?" prompt (D19/D20 withdrawn with Stage 4).
- No new report fields, radio tests, channel surveys or link tuning.
- No changes to the router, its channel or security as a requirement.
- No change to the `esp_http_server` adapter beyond P5.
- No Option B (IDF project with Arduino as a component): the two exceptions
  that motivated it are gone.

## 7. Open risks

- If the bench ends in outcome C, the fix is hardware and its schedule is
  unknown; the firmware plan still stands as written.
- `WIFI_ALL_CHANNEL_SCAN` adds about one second to each join; with the core's
  reconnect that is one second per retry while a router is away.
- Windows mDNS varies by version; the IP must always work, the name is a
  convenience.
